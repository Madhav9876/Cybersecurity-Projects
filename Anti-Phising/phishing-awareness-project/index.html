Anatomy of a Social Engineering Attack

Every phishing attack has three phases — understand them all

🎣

Input — The Bait

The delivery method and technical weaponization of the disguise. Covers phishing types, domain spoofing, lookalike URLs, and multi-channel lures.

🧠

Process — The Psychology

The cognitive triggers that force a user to bypass logic. Authority, Urgency, Curiosity, and Fear/Greed are the four primary psychological exploits.

🔐

Output — The Defense

Architecting the defense and testing the perimeter. The golden rule: Pause, Verify, Report — always through an out-of-band channel.

Phishing Hierarchy

From mass delivery to high-value executive targeting

🌊 Mass Phishing

Generic lures mimicking ubiquitous brands (Amazon, PayPal). High volume, low personalization. Roughly 1% engagement rate.

🎯 Spear Phishing

Contextual precision. Attackers use OSINT (like LinkedIn) to reference specific project names, colleagues, or internal jargon to build believability.

🐋 Whaling

Targeting the C-Suite. Focuses on founders and executives to execute high-value wire transfers (BEC) or acquire M&A documents.

Threat Vectors Beyond the Inbox

Attackers exploit every communication channel

📱

Smishing (SMS)

Malicious links delivered via text, often disguised as package deliveries or urgent bank alerts.

📞

Vishing (Voice)

Live calls utilizing caller ID spoofing to impersonate IT support or government agencies.

⬛

Quishing (QR Codes)

Malicious codes placed on physical posters or in digital PDFs, forcing users onto mobile devices where spoofed domains are harder to spot.

🔎

Search Engine Phishing

SEO poisoning that manipulates search results to place malicious lookalike websites above legitimate login portals.
